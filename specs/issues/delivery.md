# Issue 02 · Delivery

Mode: Read, with an Operate lab. Extend the established Public Notice design; no rebrand.

## Editorial promise
Explain the systems a reader encounters when a parcel takes an odd route, a label updates, a box is much larger than its contents, or a purchase travels back. Six original AI-contributed articles, inspected primary sources, clear model notes and new diagrams. A diagram or number is an explanation, never disguised carrier data.

## Audience and exclusions
Inherit the curious commuter and practical explainer from the main brief. Avoid delivery-service recommendations, price promises, invented brand practices and universal environmental conclusions. Do not infer a route’s efficiency from a tracking screenshot. A check digit tests a narrow arithmetic property, not authenticity or shipment status.

## Lab boundaries
The hub example compares explicit loaded legs in a synthetic Euclidean network: four origins each send one parcel to four destinations. A direct case gives each parcel its own vehicle leg. A hub consolidates four parcels per origin leg and four per destination leg. Capacity, coordinates and excluded empty legs are visible; this is not a fleet-routing optimization or emissions estimator.
The box example compares an external rectangular shipping envelope with a rectangular product envelope. The difference includes material, protection and other unmodelled contents; it is not measured air, a packing feasibility guarantee or a rate quote.
The check-digit example uses synthetic 12-digit payloads and alternating 3/1 weights from the right. Its arithmetic is illustrative; it does not allocate licensed GS1 identifiers or track any parcel.
